## Q1. Retention (D1/D7/D30) by genre on Android, plus session length & sessions/day

### Takeaway
The median mobile game in 2025 kept ~22% of players on D1, <4% on D7 and <1% on D30 (GameAnalytics); top-quartile games reach ~30% D1 / 6–7% D7, with Android running a few points below iOS. Puzzle/match/board/card/casino genres retain best long-term; arcade/hyper-casual retain well on D1 but collapse. Median sessions are short (~3–3.5 min, ~3.8–3.9 sessions/day), while strategy/action sessions are far longer.

### Cited Findings
**Overall benchmarks (GameAnalytics)**
- GameAnalytics' 2026 Mobile & PC Benchmarks (covering 2025 data): median D1 ≈ 22%, D7 just under 4%, D30 ≈ 0.7–0.8%. Genre-level cuts were reportedly *not* included in this edition. — [GameAnalytics 2026 report](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks); [InvestGame PDF mirror](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-2026-mobile-pc-benchmarks_compressed.pdf); summary via [gamegrowthadvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)
- Top 25% of games: D1 just above 30%; top 10%: D1 ≈ 40%; top 25% D7 in the 6–7% range (secondary write-up of GameAnalytics data, sponsored by PWN Games). — [GameDevReports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)
- Platform split, top-quartile D1: iOS 31–33% vs **Android 25–27%** (attributed to GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks). — [gamegrowthadvisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/); [segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies). *Conflict:* this does not reconcile cleanly with the "top 25% just above 30%" all-platform figure above (the blend of Android and iOS could explain it).

Qualitative genre findings: **Arcade** has outstanding D1 but struggles long-term; **Board, Card, Puzzle, Casino** show consistently strong D1, D7 and D28. — [GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks); [PDF](https://files.gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/mobile-gaming-benchmarks-2025.pdf); [Gamigion summary](https://www.gamigion.com/mobile-gaming-benchmarks-2025/)

- GameAnalytics (2025 data): median (P50) session length 3.1–3.5 min; median 3.8–3.9 sessions/day throughout the year. — [GameAnalytics 2026 report](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks)
- Top 25% titles average ~5.2 min/session; top 10% ~8 min. — [GameDevReports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)

### Inferences
- A realistic "good" target for a small dev's Android casual/puzzle game: D1 ≥ 30%, D7 ≥ 10%, D30 ≥ 4–5% (top quartile for puzzle/match). Median-level (22/4/<1) is not commercially viable for paid UA.
- Genre choice matters for the D30 ceiling: puzzle/match/board/card/tabletop sustain longest; arcade/hyper-casual front-load retention and churn fast.
- Short, frequent sessions (3–5 min, 4+ per day) fit casual puzzle; strategy trades frequency for long sessions and deep monetization.

### Gaps
- No primary, open, genre-by-genre Android D1/D7/D30 table with median + top quartile for 2025–2026 was accessible (GameAnalytics dropped genre cuts in the 2026 edition; PDFs gated). Adjust/AppsFlyer 2025 genre retention tables not found.
- Session-length definitions differ across sources (per-session vs per-day), making cross-source comparisons unreliable.

## Q2. ARPDAU, ARPPU, payer conversion, LTV; IAP vs ads vs hybrid mix; Android CPI by genre & region

### Takeaway
ARPDAU scales sharply with genre depth: hyper-casual ~$0.01–0.08, casual/puzzle ~$0.05–0.15 (top $0.20–0.30), hybrid-casual ~$0.15–0.50, mid-core/strategy $0.25–$1+. Android CPIs are much cheaper than iOS but also return less (Liftoff: casual CPI $0.14 Android vs $1.41 iOS; D30 ROAS 15% vs 47%). Hybrid (IAP + ads) monetization outperforms single-stream models in AppsFlyer data. Payer-conversion-by-genre benchmarks were not found in open sources.

### Cited Findings
**ARPDAU (secondary sources; no open primary dataset found)**
- Hyper-casual $0.01–0.04 (one source) / $0.03–0.08 (another); Casual (puzzle, match-3) $0.05–0.15, top performers $0.20–0.30; Mid-core $0.08–0.25; Hardcore/strategy $0.25–$1.00+; Hybrid-casual $0.15–0.50 blended. — [Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games); [Perkox 2026](https://blog.perkox.com/2026/08/arpdau-benchmarks-2026/); [AdReact](https://adreact.com/blog/arpdau-calculator-benchmark-guide/)
- US/Western Europe ARPDAU is significantly higher than high-download markets like India/SEA. — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games)
- Appodeal Mobile Casual Benchmarks Report 2025 covers ad ARPU by subgenre for ~10,000 US Android games (ad ARPU, not ARPDAU). — [App Developer Magazine](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-2025/)

**Payer conversion / ARPPU / LTV**
- No reliable genre-level payer-conversion or ARPPU benchmark found in open sources; only passing references to ~1–2% payer conversion for individual games. — (gap; see [Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre))

**Revenue mix: IAP vs ads vs hybrid**
- Mid-core/strategy/RPG are IAP-led; AppsFlyer measures roughly 90% of revenue from purchases in those genres. — [Playio / AppsFlyer cited](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games)
- Approx. mix by genre (2026 guide, secondary): Hardcore/mid-core 60–80% IAP / 20–40% ads; Casual ~40–60% each; Puzzle ~70% IAP; Hyper-casual 5–15% IAP / 85–95% ads. — [gamegrowthadvisor hybrid guide](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-06-02-hybrid-monetization-mobile-games-iap-ads-guide-2026/)
- AppsFlyer (via gamegrowthadvisor): three-stream hybrid games ≈ 35% IAP / 56% ads / 7% subscription; hybrid used by <30% of games (casual 33%, hyper-casual 32%, mid-core 15%). **Android mid-core hybrid games: 146% D90 ROAS vs 93% IAP-only vs 58% ads-only.** — [gamegrowthadvisor F2P models](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-02-f2p-monetization-models-comparison-2026/)
- Statista (GDC data, 2025) chart of IAP vs IAA by genre: block puzzle only partly IAP, MOBA almost entirely IAP (exact % not retrieved). — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1616353/iap-vs-iaa-revenue-mobile-gaming-genres/)
- Market context: Sensor Tower put 2025 mobile game IAP revenue at ~$82B, ~+1% YoY, with downloads declining; **Strategy was the only category to grow revenue, downloads and time spent**; Last War: Survival and Whiteout Survival were the top-grossing games of 2025. — [Sensor Tower via Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/mobile-games-82-billion-revenue-2025-sensor-tower-report); [Sensor Tower Dec 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-december-2025)

**CPI (Android) by genre & region**

Avg casual **D30 ROAS 15% Android vs 47% iOS**; on iOS Sports 80%, Kids 68%, Strategy 60%. 28% of casual installs from non-gaming publishers come from utility apps. — [Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/); [Liftoff highlights](https://liftoff.ai/blog/highlights-2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/); [Games Press](https://www.gamespress.com/28-of-casual-game-installs-from-non-gaming-publishers-come-from-utilit)
- Liftoff mid-core (12-month avg, via secondary): $0.73 Android / $3.65 iOS; LatAm mid-core CPI $0.27 vs North America $5.45. — [gamegrowthadvisor CPI](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/); [megadigital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/cpi-mobile-game-guide/)
- Tier-1 (US) Android ranges (secondary, partly attributed to Admiral Media 2026): Hyper-casual $0.30–1.20; Casual/Puzzle $1.20–3.00 (others say $1.50–3.50); Idle RPG $2.00–5.00; Mid-core/Strategy $4.00–10.00. — [FoxData 2026](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/); [Playio CPI](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-cpi-benchmarks-2026); [gamegrowthadvisor CPI](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/)
- AppsFlyer State of Gaming 2023 (dated, global): Android CPI Match $1.45, Casino $1.14, RPG $3.04, Simulation $0.66, Puzzle $0.69. — [gamegrowthadvisor CPI](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/)
- Generic app CPI 2026: iOS $2.24, Android $1.12 (all apps, low-quality source). — [The Social Outline](https://thesocialoutline.com/blog/mobile-app-cpi-benchmarks-2026)
- *Conflict:* Liftoff's $0.14 Android casual blend (global, heavy low-cost regions) vs $1.20–3.50 US-Tier-1 estimates differ by ~10x — the difference is regional mix, not contradiction per se.

### Inferences
- For a small team with limited UA budget on Android: casual puzzle/block/match with hybrid monetization (rewarded ads + IAP removal/boosters/pass) is the best fit — cheap installs, high D30 ceiling, and ad revenue monetizes the 98%+ non-payers.
- Strategy/4X has the highest revenue but US Android CPIs of $4–10+ and heavy live-ops/content needs — poor fit for a small team.
- LTV rule of thumb: LTV ≈ ARPDAU × average lifetime days; at $0.10 ARPDAU you need ~20+ lifetime days per install to beat a $2 US Android CPI — demanding top-quartile retention.
- Android-first launches can soft-launch cheaply in LatAm/SEA (very low CPIs) to validate retention before Tier-1 spend.

## Q3. Core loops & meta systems that drive retention (progression, collection, daily rewards, streaks, passes, live ops, social, PvP, roguelite, merge/idle, variable rewards)

### Takeaway
The evidence favors a polished, satisfying core loop first with a *light* meta (Royal Match), plus a power-progression spine (hybrid-casual), layered with daily rewards, events and passes. At the top of the grossing charts, alliance/social live ops and rotating reward passes drive revenue (Last War, Whiteout Survival, Pokémon GO). Rigorous causal evidence for individual mechanics (near-miss, streaks, variable rewards) is thin; stacking them amplifies pull and draws regulatory scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
- **Royal Match:** launched with a simplified meta (earn stars by completing levels → spend stars on tasks/areas, "puzzle & decorate lite"), and A/B-tested even lighter metas including a no-meta control; the meta provides a long-term progression vector but never overshadows the core puzzle. Boosters/bombs blast through early blockers for satisfying early levels. — [Naavik: Royal Match success through iteration](https://naavik.co/digest/royal-match-finding-success-through-iteration/); [Naavik deep dive](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/royal-match/); [Deconstructor of Fun 2021](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2021/3/21/royal-match-the-new-king-from-turkey); [Naavik: The Royal Blueprint](https://naavik.co/digest/why-dream-games-success-is-a-challenge-to-replicate/)
- **Hybrid-casual:** "practically all hybridcasual hits have a power progression that is used as the main thread to build the meta progression onto." — [Naavik: Evolution of Hybridcasual](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/evolution-of-hybridcasual-deepdive/)
- **Block/arrow puzzle genre:** most titles build puzzle core and meta as separate systems stitched together afterwards rather than one loop (secondary, citing a Jan 2026 DoF analysis). — [emhance.ai](https://www.emhance.ai/blog/arrow-puzzle-genre-breakdown-report)
- **Live ops & social at the top:** Last War: Survival held #1 revenue through much of H2 2025, credited to alliance-based live ops and rotating reward passes; a Season 3 Battle Pass, exclusive hero weapons and a recurring "Gold Brick" event drove a March 2025 spike. Pokémon GO's Nov 2025 growth tied to a dense event slate incl. GO Pass. Sensor Tower: market shifting toward retention and live ops as downloads dip. — [Sensor Tower Oct 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-october-2025); [Sensor Tower Mar 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-march-2025); [Sensor Tower Nov 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-november-2025); [Outlook Respawn on Sensor Tower](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/mobile-games-82-billion-revenue-2025-sensor-tower-report)
- **Daily login rewards:** used by 95% of (Korean) mobile games studied; mechanism is loss aversion — missing a day feels like losing accumulated progress. Descriptive, not a measured effect. — [arXiv 2504.10714 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10714)
- **Stacked mechanics:** a Media Psychology study (summarized by a blog; original not verified) found reward systems did not by themselves cause problem gaming, but stacking streaks, timers, variable prizes and loss-framed messages compounds the pull beyond any single mechanic. — [Nerdbot summary](https://nerdbot.com/2026/08/18/the-psychology-behind-daily-rewards-in-video-games/)
- **Streak adoption:** streak screens rose from 6.8% of captured apps (2023) to 14.0% (2024), then fell to 8.1% (2025); streaks work where a genuine daily-habit loop exists. — [Lazyweb research](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/are-streaks-still-spreading-beyond-duolingo.md)
- Weekly challenges and daily rewards are claimed to have an outsized effect on D7 retention (vendor blog, unquantified). — [gamegrowthadvisor LiveOps](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-31-liveops-strategy-mobile-games-guide/)

### Inferences
- Recommended stack for a small Android casual title: (1) instantly readable, juicy core puzzle with short levels; (2) light progression meta (stars → decorate/collect, or power progression for hybrid-casual); (3) daily reward calendar with gentle (non-punitive) streaks; (4) weekly rotating events + a monthly season/pass; (5) lightweight async social (leaderboards, team chests) later. Roguelite runs and merge/idle are proven hybrid-casual cores but need an equally strong power-progression spine.
- Heavy PvP/guild warfare (4X model) drives the biggest revenue but requires scale (servers, community, content) beyond a small team.

### Gaps
- No GameRefinery 2025–2026 feature-adoption data (e.g., % of top-grossing games with battle passes) was retrievable.
- No controlled/public studies quantifying the retention lift of near-miss design, variable rewards or streaks in mobile games; no published Duolingo streak data found.

## Q4. Successful live ops for a small team (event cadence, seasons)

### Takeaway
Industry guidance converges on daily/weekly lightweight touchpoints, a meaningful event every ~2 weeks (2–4 weeks for casual), and 4–8-week seasons/passes — built from reusable event templates with content prepared ahead and rest weeks to avoid fatigue. Most guidance is vendor-blog quality.

### Cited Findings
- Mobile cadence: daily & weekly events with seasons of 4–8 weeks. — [Innovecs Games](https://www.innovecsgames.com/blog/liveops-strategy-for-games/)
- A major event every ~14 days with daily/weekly touchpoints (login bonuses, rotating challenges) between; casual titles: a meaningful event or content drop every 2–4 weeks. — [gamegrowthadvisor LiveOps](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-31-liveops-strategy-mobile-games-guide/); [FirstLook.gg](https://firstlook.gg/blog/live-ops-101-player-retention/)
- Monthly-only events create dry spells where retention drops; a weekly program needs ~2x the content throughput of a monthly one. Big casual/puzzle titles run 15–25 overlapping event layers per month (upper bound, not small-team realistic). — [gamegrowthadvisor LiveOps](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-31-liveops-strategy-mobile-games-guide/); [Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/tips-to-build-a-winning-liveops-strategy-for-games)
- Small-team tactics: reusable event frameworks to lower per-event cost; keep 2–3 events ready ahead; build rest weeks; rotate event types; don't stack events competing for the same resource; schedule strongest events at typical churn points on the retention curve. — [gamegrowthadvisor LiveOps](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-31-liveops-strategy-mobile-games-guide/); [gamepill](https://gamepill.com/the-live-ops-retention-engine/)
- Claim (unverified): content updates every 4–6 weeks see 25–35% higher D90 retention vs 8–12-week cycles, at 40–60% higher dev cost. — [gamegrowthadvisor LiveOps](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-31-liveops-strategy-mobile-games-guide/)
- Top-grossing example: Last War's rotating reward passes + recurring events (Gold Brick) + seasonal battle pass. — [Sensor Tower Mar 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-march-2025)

### Inferences
- A feasible small-team calendar: always-on daily reward + daily quests; 2 rotating weekly event templates (e.g., score race, collection hunt) driven by server-side config; one 4-week season pass (free + premium track); 1–2 holiday events per quarter; content (new levels) every 2–4 weeks. Everything data-driven via remote config so events ship without app updates.

### Gaps
- No verified, published cadence data for specific hits (Royal Match, Block Blast); would need patch histories or paid teardowns.

## Q5. Ethical / regulatory boundaries (loot boxes, Google Play gambling rules, dark patterns, kids/COPPA/Families) and how to stay compelling without manipulation

### Takeaway
Regulation is tightening fast: Google Play requires pre-purchase loot-box odds disclosure and bans simulated gambling (social casino) content outside its rules; the FTC fined HoYoverse $20M (Jan 2025) over loot boxes/COPPA; Brazil's Digital ECA (in force 17 Mar 2026) bans/restricts loot boxes in games likely accessed by minors with fines up to R$50M or 10% of Brazil revenue; the EU CPC network launched coordinated action vs nine game companies (30 Sep 2026) over virtual currencies, and the Digital Fairness Act is expected around Q4 2026. The safe path: avoid paid random rewards (or disclose odds and age-gate), price in real money / transparent single currency, no forced currency conversion, no punitive loss-framing, and don't target kids unless fully Families-compliant.

### Cited Findings
**Google Play**
- Apps offering randomized virtual items for purchase must clearly disclose the odds before purchase (policy since May 2019, aligning with Apple's 2017 rule). — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/google-play-now-requires-disclosure-of-loot-box-odds); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/google-play-developers-must-now-disclose-loot-box-odds); [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/content/google-tightens-play-store-policies-for-loot-boxes-and-advertising-to-children)
- Real-Money Gambling, Games & Contests policy: real-money gambling only for licensed, Google-approved operators (free to download, no Play In-app Billing, block minors & unauthorized locations); "apps must not offer simulated gambling content (e.g., social casino apps; apps with virtual slot machines)" outside permitted rules; gambling ads need responsible-gambling info; pilots (e.g., daily fantasy sports in Mexico) with effective date 22 Jan 2026. Exact country list not confirmed. — [Google Play policy 9877032](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)
- Families policy: apps designed for children must comply; if target audience is children only, use only ads SDKs self-certified for Play Families; Designed for Families program merged into broader Families Policy (2022); AdMob auto-blocks non-certified ad sources in child-directed apps; gambling ads must not reach users known to be under 18. — [Play Developer Programme Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17517561?hl=en-IN); [Android Developers Blog 2022](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/11/helping-kids-and-families-find-high-quality-apps-for-kids.html); [AdMob Families help](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6223431?hl=en); [Unity Families compliance](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/grow/ads/privacy/google-families-compliance)

**US (FTC / COPPA)**
- FTC v. Cognosphere (HoYoverse, Genshin Impact), Jan 2025: $20M penalty; ban on selling loot boxes to under-16s without parental consent; must disclose loot box odds and virtual-currency exchange rates; delete under-13 data; comply with COPPA. FTC alleged confusing multi-step currency exchanges and misleading odds. HoYoverse disputed allegations. Proposed order needed court approval (final approval status not confirmed). — [FTC consumer alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/01/ftc-settlement-order-bans-sales-genshin-impact-loot-boxes-kids-under-16-without-their-parents); [FTC business blog](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/hoyoverse-agrees-to-20-million-settlement-with-ftc-over-loot-boxes/); [Esports Legal News](https://esportslegal.news/2025/11/25/ftc-vs-genshin-impact-20-million/)
- (From prior knowledge, URL not re-verified this session) FTC's Dec 2022 Epic Games settlements ($275M COPPA + $245M refunds over "dark patterns" causing unwanted purchases) remain the key US dark-pattern precedent. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/fortnite-video-game-maker-epic-games-pay-more-half-billion-dollars-over-ftc-allegations)
- (From prior knowledge, not re-verified this session) FTC finalized COPPA Rule amendments in Jan 2025 (separate opt-in parental consent for third-party data sharing/targeted ads; data retention limits), with compliance due around April 2026. — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data)

### Inferences
- "Compelling but clean" design rules for a small dev (derived from the above):
  1. No paid randomized rewards (gacha/loot boxes). Use deterministic purchases, rewarded ads for free random chests, or visible-odds free rewards. If any paid randomness exists: disclose odds, add pity/guarantees, age-gate, and geo-disable (Belgium, Brazil-minors).
  2. Single, transparent soft currency; show real-money prices; no awkward bundle sizes that leave leftovers.
  3. Non-punitive streaks (streak freezes/repair; rewards for returning, not losses for missing); avoid stacking timers + streak loss + variable prizes + loss-framed notifications.
  4. Don't build social casino/slot mechanics (Play simulated-gambling restrictions).
  5. Rate for 13+/general audience unless fully Families-compliant (certified ad SDKs, COPPA consent, no behavioral ads); avoid kid-appealing marketing if not designing for kids.
  6. Clear ad placements (rewarded, opt-in; no deceptive close buttons), easy refunds/purchase confirmations to avoid Epic-style "dark pattern" claims.
- The engagement drivers that survive regulation — satisfying core loop, deterministic progression, collection, events, passes with clear contents, cooperative social — are also the ones top hits rely on.
