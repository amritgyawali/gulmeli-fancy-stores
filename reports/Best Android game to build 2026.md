# Bet on Puzzle Roguelites, Not 4X Giants

The best Android game for a solo developer or small team to start in late 2026 is a **puzzle roguelite**. That means a simple puzzle you understand in seconds (placing blocks on a grid, clearing lines) wrapped in run-based "pick one of three upgrades" build-crafting, with a light unlock meta. It should be free on Android with opt-in rewarded ads, a one-time unlock and a season pass, and it should have a matching PC version on Steam. This pick sits where three trends meet. First, puzzle is the one big genre still growing: its in-app revenue rose about **20% in H1 2026 while the whole market fell 2%**, and puzzle games keep players longer than almost any other genre. Second, roguelite depth gives you the "advanced," replayable feel you want, and it is the style behind the few recent indie mobile wins (Vampire Survivors, Balatro) that spread without paid ads. Third, the genres that earn the most (4X strategy, RPG/gacha, battle royale, casino) are walled off by giant publishers paying $4–10 per install. Total downloads are shrinking 7–13% a year, so every new game fights for the same players, and you need a game people tell friends about rather than one you buy players for. For a Nepal-based developer, two facts shape the plan. South Asian players download a lot but spend little, so the game must be built for a global audience. You also need to confirm early that Google Play will pay out to a Nepal account. Finally, "addictive" must mean "fun to come back to," not manipulation. Regulators in the EU, US and Brazil are now fining and investigating games over loot boxes, confusing currencies and pressure tactics.

## Downloads are falling, and puzzle is the genre still climbing

Mobile gaming stopped growing. Store in-app purchase (IAP) revenue was about **$82B in 2025, up only ~1%** ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/press/press-release-sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-gaming-drove-52-billion-downloads-82b-iap-revenue-on-mobile-and-12b-premium-revenue-on-steam)). In H1 2026 it slipped to **$39.8B from $40.6B a year earlier** ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mobile-game-revenue-decline-2026/)). Downloads fell faster. Installs dropped about 7% in 2025 to 50.4B ([Cinevva summary of Sensor Tower](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/casual-games-trends-2026)), then about **12% in H1 2026** to roughly 24B ([MobileGamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-sensor-towers-h1-analysis-hay-days-comeback-julys-top-games-big-krafton-numbers-and-more/)). Naavik says that decline is "Android-driven" ([Naavik](https://naavik.substack.com/p/h1-2026-mobile-gamings-stability)). Sensor Tower's own conclusion is that growth "can no longer rely heavily on user acquisition" ([Sensor Tower via Bolsamania](https://www.bolsamania.com/nota-de-prensa/mercados/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-gaming-drove-94-billion-in-revenue-in-2025-downloads-reached-52-billion--21828650.html)). Put simply, the market is now zero-sum. A new game has to win players away from games they already play, and it has to keep them.

Inside that flat market, money is moving between genres. Strategy (mainly 4X "build a base, join an alliance, go to war" games) is still the biggest pool. But puzzle is the one with momentum, and the H1 2026 numbers show the gap closing fast.

| Genre (H1 2026, global store IAP) | Revenue | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy / 4X | **$9.3B** | About −5% YoY; 4X peaked in Q4 2025 |
| Puzzle | **$8.1B** | **About +20% YoY**, fastest-growing major genre |
| RPG | $6.1B | Declining |
| Casino | $5.0B | Not reported |

Sources: [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/mobile-game-revenue-decline-2026/); [GameDev Reports, Sensor Tower H1'26](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-gaming-market-in).

4X revenue peaked at **$3.09B in Q4 2025** and has fallen for two quarters since. A Sensor Tower analyst says its growth is "tapering off" ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-gaming-market-in); [Naavik podcast](https://naavik.co/podcast/the-state-of-gaming-in-2026-with-sensor-tower/)). Mid-core games are losing revenue, while **hybrid-casual** is growing ([Naavik](https://naavik.substack.com/p/h1-2026-mobile-gamings-stability)). Hybrid-casual means a simple, snackable core with progression and in-app purchases layered on top. It grew about **20% to ~$4.2B in 2025** ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)) and about **23% to ~$2.4B in H1 2026** ([Playio citing Sensor Tower](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-genres-list)). For Android developers specifically, Google Play game revenue edged up in Q1 2026 while iOS game revenue fell ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-market-in-q1)).

The breakout hits tell the same story. **Block Blast!**, a block puzzle, was the world's #1 game by downloads in Q1 2026 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423998721/en/Block-Blast-Ends-Q1-2026-as-the-No.-1-Most-Downloaded-Mobile-Game-Worldwide)), and it was #1 again on Google Play in August 2026 with about 25M installs ([FoxData](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/global-mobile-game-rankings-august-2026-top-15-downloads-revenue/)). By June 2026, **three "arrow escape" puzzles were in the global top five** by downloads ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-top-mobile-games-by-revenue-6bb)). **Pixel Flow**, a colour-sorting puzzle, made about **$198M in player spending in its first year** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/loom-games-pixel-flow-makes-almost-200m-in-player-spending-during-first-year/)). Other risers include merge-plus-story games (Gossip Harbor set a revenue record in August 2026 and was #2 grossing worldwide in September, per [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-top-mobile-games-by-revenue-2a7)), cozy life-sims (Heartopia passed 12M downloads in two weeks, per [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-top-mobile-games-by-revenue-11d)) and roguelite action RPGs. Habby's Archero 2 made **$32.8M in its first 30 days** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/archero-2-makes-328m-in-first-30-days-from-player-spending/)). The common pattern is a **simple core mechanic, a progression or story layer on top, and a mix of ads and purchases**. That pattern is cheap to produce, which is why it is the most promising zone for a small team.

## Good retention means 30% tomorrow and 4% after a month

"Addictive" is measured by retention. D1 retention is the share of new players who come back the next day, and D7 and D30 work the same way for a week and a month. The typical game does badly. GameAnalytics' 2026 benchmarks put the **median game at about 22% D1, under 4% D7 and under 1% D30** ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks)). The top quarter of games reach just over **30% D1 and 6–7% D7**, and the top 10% reach about 40% D1 ([GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Android usually runs a few points below iOS, with top-quartile D1 of about 25–27% versus 31–33% ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-retention-strategies-2026/)).

Genre sets the ceiling. In GameAnalytics' genre data, **puzzle, board, card and casino games hold strong retention on D1, D7 and D28**, while arcade games start strong and then collapse ([GameAnalytics 2025](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). One less reliable table puts puzzle at roughly **32% / 12% / 5%** (D1/D7/D30), ahead of RPG and strategy ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies)). Mistplay found tabletop games led Android D30 at 5.1% ([Mistplay](https://business.mistplay.com/resources/mobile-gaming-trends-2025-report)). Sessions are short. The median is **3.1–3.5 minutes, about 3.8–3.9 times a day** ([GameAnalytics](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2026-mobile-pc-gaming-benchmarks)), and the top 10% of games average about 8 minutes ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Any game you design must work in three-minute bites.

The money side looks like this. ARPDAU is the average revenue per daily player, and CPI is the cost to buy one install with ads.

| Metric | Hyper-casual | Casual / puzzle | Hybrid-casual | Mid-core / strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARPDAU | $0.01–0.08 | $0.05–0.15 (top: $0.20–0.30) | $0.15–0.50 | $0.25–$1.00+ |
| US Android CPI | $0.30–1.20 | $1.20–3.00 | — | $4.00–10.00 |

Sources: [Juego Studio](https://www.juegostudio.com/blog/arpdau-benchmarks-by-game-genre); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games); [FoxData 2026](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/2026-mobile-game-user-acquisition-cost-benchmarks-how-much-should-you-spend/). These are secondary blog figures, so treat them as directional.

Android installs are cheap worldwide but return less. Liftoff found the average casual CPI was **$0.14 on Android versus $1.41 on iOS**, but 30-day return on ad spend was only **15% on Android versus 47% on iOS** ([Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/2025-casual-gaming-apps-report/)). Mixing revenue streams clearly wins. AppsFlyer data shows Android mid-core games using both ads and purchases reached **146% return on ad spend by day 90, against 93% for purchase-only and 58% for ads-only** ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-02-f2p-monetization-models-comparison-2026/)). Puzzle and lifestyle hybrid-casual games earn about **59% from purchases and 41% from ads** ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)).

### Paid ads rarely pay back for a small team

Put these numbers together and you get the most important fact in this report. I fitted a simple retention curve to a top-quartile puzzle game (30% D1, 10% D7, which comes out at about 4.4% D30). On that curve, the average install plays on about **5.4 days in its first 90 days and about 9.5 days in its first year**. At a typical $0.10 ARPDAU, that install is worth about **$0.54 by day 90 and about $0.95 after a year**, against a US Android puzzle CPI of $1.20–3.00. Even at a strong hybrid ARPDAU of $0.30, a year of play is worth only about $2.84. A median game (22% D1, 4% D7) gets about three days of play per install per year. This is my own back-of-envelope estimate, not a published figure, but the conclusion holds across reasonable inputs. **Buying players in rich markets only works for top-10% games with mixed monetization and a large testing budget.** A small team's players have to come for free: from word of mouth, community, Google Play featuring, Steam, or a publisher who pays for testing. That fact drives the choice of game.

## Giants own the top of the charts; indies win on depth and word of mouth

The richest genres are effectively closed. In 2024, strategy made **only ~4% of downloads but 21.4% of revenue**, the profile of a genre that lives on big spenders and huge ad budgets ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-market-in-2025)). The top 30 Chinese publishers alone made **$23.3B in 2025** ([Sensor Tower via itiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1136036604)). Last War: Survival earned about **$1.1B in H1 2026 even after reportedly switching off paid ads** ([Singular](https://www.singular.net/blog/top-mobile-games/)). That shows how locked-in those spender communities are. Battle royale and MOBA games dominate play in Nepal, Pakistan and Bangladesh, but PUBG Mobile, Free Fire and Mobile Legends own them ([RB Gaming Center](https://rbgamingcenter.com/best-mobile-games-in-nepal/); [Arena Lobby](https://www.arenalobby.pk/top-mobile-games-pakistani-gamers-love-in-2026/)).

Casual puzzle is open, but it works like a lottery. Block Blast reached **70M daily players and ran over 10,000 A/B tests in 2025** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260108572322/en/Block-Blast-Scales-to-70M-DAU-as-Hungry-Studio-Runs-Over-10000-Experiments-in-a-Single-Year)). Its studio reportedly had nearly 1,000 staff by the end of 2025, though sources conflict ([36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/de/p/3706617203732616)). Pixel Flow's studio was valued at **over $1B when Scopely took a majority stake** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/pixel-flow-wins-game-of-the-year-at-the-pocket-gamer-mobile-games-awards-2026/)). Voodoo builds about **2,000 prototypes a year** to find a few hits ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/06/02/how-voodoo-builds-hits-four-product-lessons-from-a-2000-prototypes-a-year-machine)). AI makes copying cheaper still: **over half of the ad creatives used by top-100 grossing games are AI-assisted** ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-market-landscape)). A shallow mechanic like an arrow puzzle can be cloned within weeks.

The indie wins that are well documented come from depth and community, not ad spend. **Vampire Survivors** on mobile passed **5M downloads in just over three months with no paid marketing**. Its only monetization was an optional rewarded ad at the end of a run ([MobileGamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/vampire-survivors-mobile-hits-5m-downloads/)). Its makers rushed the port because "1:1 copies with stolen code" were already flooding the stores. **Balatro**, a poker roguelite deckbuilder, grossed about **$1M in its first mobile week and over $9M by January 2025** ([Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1552648/balatro-app-revenue)). Later estimates put it at about $21M on mobile ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase)). Habby has turned a roguelite "pick a skill each level" loop with casual art into a repeatable formula, from Archero to Capybara Go to Archero 2 ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/7/31/habbys-hybridcasual-empire-the-template-that-built-a-powerhouse)). The honest baseline is still sobering. One solo developer made about **$4,000 in five months** and warned that successful indie games are "objectively just the exception" ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/analytics/item-1157)).

| Genre | 2025–26 signal | Fit for a solo / small team |
|---|---|---|
| 4X strategy | Biggest pool, past its peak, Android CPI ~$4 | **Closed**: needs huge ad budgets and live-ops teams |
| RPG / gacha | Large but shrinking | **Closed**: content-heavy, paid random rewards under legal fire |
| Battle royale / MOBA | Dominates South Asian play | **Closed**: Krafton, Garena and Moonton own it |
| Social casino | $5.0B in H1 2026 | **Closed**: very high CPI and Google Play restrictions |
| Hybrid-casual puzzle (block, arrow, sort) | Fastest growth, cheapest installs | **Open but a lottery**: won by testing speed; best via a publisher |
| Merge + story, cozy life-sim | Rising (Gossip Harbor, Heartopia) | **Hard**: needs a constant flow of new art and story |
| Roguelite / deckbuilder / survivor-like | Vampire Survivors, Balatro, Habby | **Best fit**: depth from systems, grows through community |
| Local board games (Ludo, Bagh Chal) | Huge South Asian downloads, little spending | **Good learning project, weak business** |

### South Asia gives you downloads, not revenue

India is the world's biggest download market, with **8.45B game installs in FY2024-25** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indias-mobile-games-market-booms-with-845bn-installs/)) and **15.7% of global downloads in December 2025** ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/top-10-worldwide-mobile-games-by-revenue-and-downloads-in-december-2025)). Yet its IAP revenue is only **just over $400M**, and shooters take about half of that ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/indias-mobile-games-market-booms-with-845bn-installs/)). Nepal has no proper market data. Fan blogs point to PUBG Mobile, Free Fire, Mobile Legends, and board games like Ludo and Bagh Chal on low- and mid-range phones ([Gaming Nepal](https://gamingnepal.in/the-top-8-most-popular-mobile-games-in-nepal-a-deep-dive-into-the-gaming/); [Autoncell](https://www.autoncell.com/detail/news/top-five-most-popular-mobile-games-nepal-2021)). The lesson is to build for global players, keep the game light enough for cheap phones, and treat local players as a bonus and a test audience, not your income.

One Nepal-specific risk could change the business model. Nepal is on Google's list of countries where **users** can buy paid apps ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/143779?hl=en)), but this research found **no source confirming that a Nepal-based developer can open a Play merchant account and get paid**. Check this in Play Console before you design any purchases. If it is not supported, your options are ads-only monetization, a company registered in a supported country, or a publisher who handles payments. That uncertainty is one more reason to design a game that works on rewarded ads alone and treats purchases as upside.

### Unity 6 or Godot 4.6 both work; Google Play adds three chores

Engine choice is no longer a trap. Unity cancelled its Runtime Fee, and **Unity Personal is free up to $200K in revenue or funding** ([Unity](https://unity.com/products/pricing-updates); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/09/unity-scraps-controversial-runtime-fee-but-raises-prices)). Pro rose to about $2,310 a year in January 2026, but a small team will not need it. Godot has matured on Android. Version 4.5.2 fixed many mobile rendering crashes, and Godot's team "strongly recommend[s]" Vulkan Mobile games on Google Play upgrade ([Godot](https://godotengine.org/article/maintenance-release-godot-4-5-2/)). Its on-device Android build tool went stable in June 2026 ([Godot](https://godotengine.org/article/gabe-stable-release/)). Unreal is overkill for a 2D mobile game. On the Google Play side, new apps and updates had to target **Android 16 (API 36) by 31 August 2026**, with extensions to 1 November ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)). Developer identity verification starts on **30 September 2026** in four countries and goes global in 2027 ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-sideloading-changes-timeline-3679204/)). Fees fell after the Epic settlement: from 30 June 2026, Google charges **10% on your first $1M a year in the US, UK and EEA, plus 5% if you use Play Billing** ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html)). Fees for South Asian markets after that change were not confirmed.

## Regulators now punish the tricks that made games "addictive"

The old "addiction" toolkit is now a legal risk. Google Play requires games to **show loot-box odds before purchase** and bans simulated gambling such as virtual slot machines outside its strict rules ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/google-play-developers-must-now-disclose-loot-box-odds); [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?hl=en)). In January 2025 the US FTC hit Genshin Impact's maker with a **$20M penalty** and a ban on selling loot boxes to under-16s without parental consent, citing misleading odds and confusing currency exchanges ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/01/ftc-settlement-order-bans-sales-genshin-impact-loot-boxes-kids-under-16-without-their-parents)). Brazil's Digital ECA took effect on **17 March 2026**. It restricts loot boxes in games children are likely to play, enforcement against studios began in August 2026, and fines reach **R$50M or 10% of Brazil revenue** ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/zh-hans/insights/publications/2026/04/enforcement-of-brazils-eca-digital-introduces-new-obligations-for-companies)). On **30 September 2026**, EU consumer authorities launched action against **nine companies including King, Supercell and Mojang**. It covers Candy Crush, Clash of Clans and Minecraft, and focuses on hidden real-money prices, forced currency purchases and random rewards ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/10/15/eu-consumer-protection-network-launches-action-involving-nine-video-games-compani-102o5do); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/10/01/minecraft-and-candy-crush-among-games-targeted-by-consumer-watchdog/)). The EU's Digital Fairness Act, expected around late 2026, could restrict pay-to-win, loot boxes and addictive design features ([Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102ltio/the-eus-proposed-digital-fairness-act-a-game-developers-guide-to-potential-imp)). Belgium has treated paid loot boxes as gambling since 2018 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/05/31/google-play-store-loot-boxes)).

The evidence on which mechanics actually keep players is thinner than the industry admits. Daily login rewards appear in **95% of Korean mobile games** studied, and they work through loss aversion ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10714)). A study summarized secondhand found that single reward systems do not cause problem gaming, but **stacking streaks, timers, random prizes and loss-framed messages compounds the pull** ([Nerdbot summary](https://nerdbot.com/2026/08/18/the-psychology-behind-daily-rewards-in-video-games/)). Streak screens spread fast and then pulled back, falling from 14.0% of apps in 2024 to 8.1% in 2025 ([Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/are-streaks-still-spreading-beyond-duolingo.md)). The design lessons from the biggest hits point the other way. Royal Match launched with a deliberately **light meta that never overshadows the puzzle** ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/royal-match-finding-success-through-iteration/)). And "practically all hybridcasual hits" hang their meta on a clear **power-progression spine** rather than on tricks ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/evolution-of-hybridcasual-deepdive/)).

So the safe rules are also the smart ones. Sell nothing random. If chests exist, they are free or earned through rewarded ads, with visible odds. Use one soft currency, always show real-money prices, and avoid bundle sizes that leave awkward leftovers. Make streaks forgiving, with freezes and "welcome back" rewards instead of punishments. Skip casino mechanics. Rate the game 13+ unless you fully meet Google's Families rules ([Google Play Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17517561?hl=en-IN)). Make every ad opt-in, with honest close buttons. A roguelite fits these rules unusually well, because its random rewards (new upgrade offers each round) come **from playing, not from paying**. You get the "one more run" pull without the legal exposure.

## The build: a puzzle roguelite with three-minute rounds and a Steam twin

The recommendation combines the strongest signals in the data. It uses puzzle's growth, retention and cheap installs. It adds roguelite depth for word of mouth and the "advanced" feel. It uses mixed ads and purchases, the model that earns most on Android. And it stays clean of the regulatory crackdown. It also plays to a small team's strengths: the depth comes from **systems (how upgrades combine), not from a mountain of art and levels**, so one good programmer-designer can make it rich.

### Core loop: place, clear, draft, repeat

A **round** lasts two to four minutes, which matches the median three-minute session. The player gets three pieces at a time and places them on an 8×8 grid. Clearing rows, columns or colour groups scores points, and the goal is to reach the round's target score before the pieces run out. After each round the player **drafts one of three "charms"** that bend the rules. Examples: clearing two lines at once triples the score, red tiles count double, every fifth piece is a bomb, or the board gains gravity. Three rounds make a **floor**. Each floor ends with a **boss round** that adds a twist, such as frozen cells, a shrinking board or no rotations. A full **run** of about eight floors takes 20–30 minutes and saves after every round, so it fits the bus ride as well as the evening session. Failing a target ends the run. The depth comes from **build archetypes** (combo builds, colour builds, bomb builds), which give skilled players something to master and give streamers something to show. The aim is a game a 10-year-old understands in five seconds, where a strategist still finds new combinations after 100 hours.

### Meta systems: unlock variety, not just power

Every run, win or lose, earns XP on an **unlock track** that adds new charms, piece sets, boards and starting characters. That track is the progression spine hybrid-casual hits rely on. Keep permanent stat boosts small so skill stays central. A **codex** records every charm and synergy discovered, which feeds the urge to collect. **Difficulty tiers** unlock after the first win and give long-term goals to the advanced players who spread word of mouth. A **daily seeded run** gives everyone the same board and charms and has a global leaderboard. It is cheap social play, and the results make natural short-video clips. Live ops follow a small-team cadence built from reusable templates ([Game Growth Advisor](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-31-liveops-strategy-mobile-games-guide/); [Innovecs](https://www.innovecsgames.com/blog/liveops-strategy-for-games/)). That means daily quests and a forgiving daily gift, two rotating **weekly mutator events** driven by server config so no app update is needed, a **four-week season** with free and premium tracks, and new charms or a new boss every two to four weeks. Friend leaderboards and "ghost" runs can come after launch.

### Monetization: ads first, purchases as upside

On Android the game is free. Revenue comes first from **opt-in rewarded ads**: one revive per run, an extra reroll of the charm draft, or double end-of-run XP. That mirrors the single, respected end-of-run ad that let Vampire Survivors keep players' goodwill. On top come a one-time **"Supporter" purchase** that removes all ads and adds a cosmetic set, a **premium season track** with cosmetics and alternate unlock paths but no power that free players can't earn, and fixed-price **cosmetic packs** (board themes, tile skins). There are no loot boxes, there is one soft currency, and every price shows in real money. On Steam the same game sells as a premium title, which builds the community and press that a mobile launch can't buy. A realistic blended goal is **$0.10–0.20 ARPDAU**, at the lower end of the hybrid-casual range. Because the model works on ads alone, it survives even if Nepal merchant payouts turn out to be unavailable.

### Tech stack, team and budget

Use **Unity 6 Personal** as the default. It is free under $200K, and most ad mediation and payment plugins support Unity first. Choose **Godot 4.6** instead if you already know it or lean toward a premium PC-first release. Both handle 2D well. Add Firebase for analytics, crash reports and Remote Config (which runs events and A/B tests without app updates), AdMob or a mediation layer for rewarded ads, Google Play Billing where payouts are available, and Google Play Games Services or a simple backend for daily leaderboards. Test on low-end Android phones from day one, and keep the download small. The core team is one programmer-designer plus a part-time 2D artist, with AI tools used for concept art drafts and ad creatives. Software costs are close to zero. The real costs are art, a few test phones, and small paid test campaigns of a few hundred dollars to measure retention. A realistic timeline for one or two people is 9–12 months to global launch. That is my estimate, not a sourced figure.

### Launch plan with kill points

| Phase | Rough timing | What you do | Move on when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Paperwork | Weeks 0–2 | Confirm Play merchant support for Nepal; finish developer verification; target API 36; open an ad account | You have a confirmed way to get paid, or you have chosen a fallback |
| 1. Find the fun | Months 1–2 | Build 2–3 rough versions of the round and draft; playtest with 20–50 people; post short clips | Testers replay without being asked |
| 2. Vertical slice + audience | Months 3–5 | One full run, ~30 charms, daily seed; Steam page; weekly devlogs on TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Reddit; a Discord server | Followers and wishlists are growing |
| 3. Test launch | Months 6–8 | Play Console closed, then open testing in low-CPI markets (South Asia, LatAm, SEA); small paid tests; watch analytics | **D1 ≥ 30% (27% is acceptable on Android), D7 ≥ 10%, D30 ≥ 4%** |
| 4. Global launch | Months 9–12 | Launch Android and Steam in the same window; pitch Google Play editorial and Play Pass; reach out to puzzle and roguelite streamers | — |
| 5. Live ops | Ongoing | Weekly events, four-week seasons, new content every 2–4 weeks | — |

The phase 3 targets are top-quartile puzzle numbers, the level where the economics above start to work. If the game misses them after two or three rounds of changes, stop and rethink the core rather than pouring in more content. **Plan B**, if you have a 2–5 person team with strong analytics skills, is the publisher route. Build cheap hybrid-casual puzzle prototypes and submit them to publishers like Voodoo, which runs **free test campaigns from day one** and scales only the winners ([Voodoo](https://voodoo.io/news/voodoo-s-secret-sauce-from-0-to-250m-hybridcasual-revenue-in-3-years)). It is a lottery ticket with a bigger prize but much worse odds.

## Conclusion

The real bottleneck for a small team in 2026 is getting players, not building the game. Shrinking downloads and payback math that only works for top-10% games mean the winning question is not "which genre earns most?" but "which game will players bring their friends to?" That changes what "advanced" should mean. For a solo developer it should mean deep systems that reward mastery, not big 3D worlds or MMO features. Depth is the one advantage a small team can afford that a 1,000-person clone factory can't copy in a month. The regulatory wave also quietly favours small, honest games. When King, Supercell and Genshin Impact's maker are being investigated over currencies and loot boxes, a game with no paid randomness and real-money prices is both lower-risk and easier to sell to players, press and Google's editors.

Three uncertainties remain. Most figures here come from search summaries of paywalled trackers, which disagree with each other by several points. Those trackers do not count ad revenue, so ad-heavy puzzle games are probably bigger than the charts show. And Nepal merchant eligibility is unconfirmed, though one look in Play Console settles it. None of these changes the core call: a deep, honest, short-session puzzle roguelite, launched on Android and Steam together and grown through community, is the best bet available to a small team right now.
